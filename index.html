II. Four PBL Phases

A structured journey from launch and inquiry to knowledge building, prototyping, and exhibition.

Phase 1: Launch & Inquiry Week 1

Entry Event: Establish real-world context; screen culture shock scenarios (crossing streets, street dining etiquette).

Grouping & Brainstorming: Form teams of 4–5 members, assign roles (Project Manager, Content Writer, Designer, Presenter), and select focus areas.

Research Methodology: Plan primary and secondary data collection; draft 3–5 survey/interview questions using Unit 2 language structures.

Phase 2: Build Knowledge and Skills Week 2

Filter and Analyze: Review research data and score information using frequency, challenge level, and practicality rubrics.

Transform Facts into Solutions: Turn raw data into actionable Do's and Don'ts tips.

Language Skills: Master polite imperative structures ("Please remember to..."), explanatory expressions, and topic vocabulary.

Phase 3: Design, Critique, and Refine Products Week 3

Creating the First Draft: Combine core text, visual elements, and digital formats suitable for printing and school Facebook publication.

Peer Critique: Evaluate posters using protocol-driven feedback focusing on clarity, language accuracy, and visual appeal.

Phase 4: Exhibition and Reflection Week 4

Gallery Walk & Showcase: Present and distribute finalized posters to incoming international students and field Q&A defenses.

Dual-Dimension Reflection: Cast Outward (content efficacy) and Cast Inward (language growth and success skills).

IV. Summative Assessment Rubric

Evaluation criteria for student project deliverables (4-point scale).

Criteria & Weight Level 1: Below Standard (1pt) Level 2: Developing (2pts) Level 3: At Standard (3pts) Level 4: Above Standard (4pts)
Content & Solution Efficacy [30%] Fails to address culture shock; inaccurate information. Basic tips, generic recommendations. Accurate, practical advice. Insightful, practical, and culturally nuanced.
English Language Proficiency [30%] Very limited vocabulary; frequent grammatical errors. Basic vocabulary with occasional errors. Accurate use of topic vocabulary and grammar. Advanced fluency and flawless expression.
Public Presentation & Q&A [20%] No eye contact; reads entirely from notes. Infrequent eye contact; relies heavily on script. Good eye contact and clear voice. Outstanding poise and convincing defense.
Poster Design [20%] Cluttered or disorganized layout. Functional layout but visually plain. Professional design and strong hierarchy. Outstanding visual design and icon usage.
